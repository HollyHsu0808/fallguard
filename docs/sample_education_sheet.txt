If a fall does happen again, the way you help them up matters.

## If they fall again and cannot get up
Do not pull them up by the arms. Check for pain, bleeding or confusion first; if any is present, or they cannot move, keep them warm with a blanket and call 000. If they are uninjured, guide them slowly: roll onto the side, come up onto hands and knees, crawl to a sturdy chair, kneel facing it, bring one foot forward, push up to sit on the chair, and rest before standing. Consider a personal alarm pendant with fall detection so help can be called from the floor.
(Source: WA Health, Health advice following a fall; Stay On Your Feet (WA))

The first weeks after a hospital stay need some extra attention.

## After coming home from hospital
Follow the discharge summary and take medicines exactly as listed; ask the pharmacist if anything is unclear. Book a GP review within a week and ask about a physiotherapy or falls-clinic referral and a medicines review. New confusion, sleepiness or a sudden change in behaviour after a hospital stay needs a same-day medical review. Check the skin over the tailbone, heels and hips daily if the person spends long periods sitting or lying.
(Source: WA Health, Health advice following a fall; NSW Health falls prevention resources)

Small changes at home and in daily routines can lower the risk of the next fall.

## Reducing the chance of another fall
Remove loose rugs and cables from walkways; add night lights between bed and bathroom; wear firm, non-slip shoes rather than slippers; install grab rails by the toilet and shower; have vision and hearing checked; ask the GP or pharmacist to review medicines that cause dizziness or sleepiness; keep up strength and balance exercise; drink enough fluids through the day.
(Source: NSW Health / healthdirect falls prevention advice; Stay On Your Feet)

Keep these contacts somewhere easy to find.

## Who to call
Call 000 if the person is unresponsive, cannot get up, has severe pain, a suspected fracture, or any head-injury warning sign. For advice at any hour call healthdirect on 1800 022 222 to speak with a registered nurse. See the GP for follow-up within a week of any fall.
(Source: healthdirect helpline; NSW Ambulance)
